Customizing Tips & Tricks

One of my favorite parts of this hobby is getting to customize my figures and models. Here are some techniques that can help you get started with your own customs.

Painting & Weathering

Painting is the go to method for customizing a figure. Whether you want your figure to look unique, add in missing details, or make it look like its been through a war, painting is the way to do it.

Personally, I like for my figures to look battle worn, which I achieve through a process called weathering. This can be achieved using drybrushing and washes.

To start, I like to begin with a wash over the figure. To do this, you add a drop of acrylic paint to a small amount of water and mix, then lather it on the figure and let it dry slightly before wiping it away with a damp cloth. For best results, use dark grays on lighter colored areas and blacks on darker areas. On browns, I always recommend using a darker shade of brown.

After I apply my wash, I like to spray my figure with a matte coat. My personal preference is using Alclad lacquer varnish in my airbrush, but that isnt very budget friendly to most beginners. In my opinion, the best rattlecan option is Matte Mr. Super Clear Finishing Spray, as it has finer particles than most alternatives. When spraying, make sure to sweep the can across the painting area instead of holding it stable, and keep it about 9 to 10 inches away from the paint surface.

Once that matte coat dries, go ahead and get either a piece of sponge or a thicker brush and dampen it. Next youre going to dip the very edge in your acrylic paint, and then wipe as much of it off the brush/sponge as you can on a paper towel. You'll then lightly swipe it across and dab it on the areas you think would look best scuffed up, like harder edges or areas that would realisticly come in contact with hard surfaces. As far as colors go, I like to start off with black, then go over it with some silver to add depth on armor pieces. On things that are meant to be leather, I do a slightly lighter shade of brown to bring out an aged appearance. Dont worry if you mess up, as a wet toothpick can clean it up.

When youre satisfied with your result, you can lock it in with another topcoat. Depending on your look, you may want either matte or satin for your finish.

Panel Lining

Ever notice how mecha model kits have so many little grooves? those are meant to represent separations in the armor panels and when theyre built out of box they can look a little off.

To really make your kits stand out on your shelf, you can panel line them. The two main methods of panel lining are fluid and markers.

I personally use markers when I want a build to look a bit more worn, as they wipe away and can leave some nice artificial shadows.

If you want your kit to look like it came right off the assembly line, I recommend panel lining fluid. The fluid is a lot cleaner looking and uses capillary action to fill in grooves without much effort, but the main drawback is that it can crack bare plastic (especially ABS).

To clean the markers, you can use your finger or you can use a q-tip dampened with isopropyl alcohol, although I would be careful as this can remove paint. The q-tip method also works for cleaning up panel lining fluid.

As far as colors go, I recommend gray on white parts and black on all others. If you want a particularly dirty look, go for brown on yellow parts.

Modifications

Ever wanted your Spider-Man figure to stick to walls like the real deal? Turns out its pretty easy to achieve. (Okay well maybe not all walls but anything magnetic works)

To do this, all you need are some small neodymium magnets (roughly 3mm width) and a good hand drill that matches that width.

To start, mark out where you want your magnets to go on the feet. I reccomend the heels and near the toes. You will then drill into the foot, but youll need to be careful not to drill past the height of the magnets so check frequently to ensure they fit well while drilling.

After you have your holes, go ahead and place the magnets you plan to use on a metal surface, as this ensures their polarities dont contradict each other.

Once youve got your magnets ready, add a drop of any superglue to the tops of the magntes and place the holes in the feet over them.

Wait for the glue to dry and enjoy your new Spider-Man shaped fridge magnet!
